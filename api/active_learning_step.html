active_learning_step.check module#

seamm-al check: does the committee’s spread predict its real error?

For a trained committee and labelled frames, every frame gets its committee spreads (rho_*, as seamm-al score) and its out-of-fold errors, the errors of the members that never trained on it:

  • a frame held out by one member (its fold): that member’s error;

  • a frame no member trained on (a common validation or test set): the mean of the members’ errors;

  • a frame every member trained on is trained and left out of the statistics.

The errors are the force RMSE over the atoms (err_forces, eV/A, the RMS of the force-vector error), the energy per atom (err_energy, eV) and, for cells, the atomic and molecular pressures (err_pressure, err_pressure_molecular, atm). The label’s molecular pressure is computed from its stress and forces with molecular_pressure(). Alongside, the worst-member errors of Smith et al.’s definition (worst_error(): eps_energy per sqrt(atom), eps_forces, eps_pressure, eps_pressure_molecular) over all members, which --calibrate uses.

The committee directory is either one made by seamm-al train (manifest.json and frames.csv: frame i is held out by member fold[i], matched by configuration name) or one with a fold_manifest.json per member, listing tags under held_out, validation and test (mlff’s layout). tag_key names the info key holding a frame’s tag; by default its configuration name.

Outputs: a CSV with one row per frame, and the Spearman correlations of each spread with its error, per kind, over all out-of-fold frames and over the held-out ones only.

active_learning_step.committee module#

Query-by-committee tools: ensembles of potentials, disagreement, thresholds.

The active-learning recipe of Smith et al. (J. Chem. Phys. 148, 241733, 2018): a committee of L potentials trained on different folds of the same data with different initialisations; a structure i is uncertain when the spread of the committee’s energies

\[\rho_i = \sigma_i / \sqrt{N_i}\]

(their eq 1; \(\sigma_i\) the standard deviation of the L energies, \(N_i\) the atom count) exceeds a threshold \(\hat\rho\). The threshold is calibrated on a labelled benchmark (their fig 1): it is the value that captures a chosen fraction (98 %) of the structures whose largest per-member error \(\epsilon_i = \max_l |E_l - E_{ref}| / \sqrt{N_i}\) exceeds a failure level. The mean of the L predictions is the committee’s prediction (their sec II E).

Condensed phases need more than the energy. In liquid water the energy error of clusters never predicted the pressure of the bulk, so force and pressure spreads are provided alongside, calibrated the same way:

  • \(\rho_F\): the root mean square over the atoms of the committee standard deviation of the force (eV/A);

  • \(\rho_P\): the committee standard deviation of the pressure (atm), atomic (from the stress) or molecular (the intramolecular virial removed, the pressure a rigid-molecule barostat feels).

Nothing here trains. The members are trained by the Trainer, one run per member, on the folds of kfold_indices(); Committee loads the resulting checkpoints and scores structures.

Origin and changes#

Adapted from code by Sina Mostafanejad, written for liquid water and xnn 0.8.0. The API and the formulas are his; the changes generalise it to other systems:

  • the molecules come from a covalent bond graph (molecules(), in active_learning_step.molecules) rather than atom index // 3; ions such as Li+ are molecules of their own, and the masses come from the elements (masses());

  • a structure’s total_charge reaches the models (structure_from_atoms() puts it in the structure dict that xnn’s graph builder reads);

  • Committee.from_checkpoints() refuses members that record different subtracted dispersions, as well as different cutoffs;

  • the numpy-only functions (kfold_indices(), calibrate_threshold(), split_statistics(), select()) live in active_learning_step.thresholds, so that selecting needs no torch; they are re-exported here unchanged;

  • besides rho_forces, disagreement() returns the per-atom spread rho_forces_atom and its maximum rho_forces_max_atom, because one bad ion environment is diluted in the RMS over a large cell.

xnn interface#

Written for xnn 0.11.x. The xnn names used, all public:

  • xnn.common.data: AtomicDataset, AtomicGraph, collate;

  • xnn.common.models.hub: load_checkpoint, build_potential (in Committee.from_checkpoints());

  • the checkpoint’s Config.subtracted_dispersion attribute, read with a default as xnn.common.models.registry.recorded_dispersion does;

  • members are xnn.common.models.ForceStressOutput objects, as build_potential returns them.

active_learning_step.engine module#

seamm-al engine: a committee’s mean as one MDI engine, with its spreads.

The committee mean is served to an MDI driver (LAMMPS, fix mdi/qm) as a single potential, adapted from code by Sina Mostafanejad: the mean of the members’ short-range cores is one InteratomicPotential (CommitteeMeanCore); the dispersion recorded in the checkpoints is added once on top (the same term in every member, so it is not averaged); and the force and stress heads differentiate the sum. The mean of the members’ energies has the mean of their forces as its gradient, so this is the committee’s prediction at the cost of one backward pass.

What is added here: every every evaluations (--spreads FILE --every N) the engine also evaluates each member on its own (one forward and backward pass per member, only on the steps written) and appends the committee spreads to a CSV: rho_energy, rho_forces, rho_forces_max_atom and, for periodic systems, rho_pressure and rho_pressure_molecular (molecules from the bond graph of the first frame written). Each row also carries max_dF, the largest difference between the engine’s forces and the mean of the members’ forces (zero to rounding when no dispersion is added; the check that the mean core is the members’ mean). With --flag NAME=VALUE a frame whose spread reaches a value is written, with its step and spreads and the per-atom force spread, to an extxyz (--flagged); --frames writes every frame recorded.

The members and the mean share their modules: each member is built once and the mean core holds the members’ cores.

active_learning_step.molecules module#

Molecules of a structure from its covalent bonds, with molsystem’s rule.

The committee’s molecular pressure removes the intramolecular virial, so it needs to know which atoms form each molecule. SEAMM defines molecules through bonds: molsystem’s Configuration.perceive_bonds() and find_molecules(). This module applies the same rule to coordinates alone, so that the command line runs in an MLFF environment that has neither SEAMM nor molsystem, and gives the same molecules:

  • two atoms are bonded when min_distance < d < tolerance * (r_i + r_j), with the Pyykkö covalent radii molsystem uses (covalent_radii) and tolerance 1.2, using every periodic image (ASE’s neighbour list);

  • the alkali and alkaline-earth metals (IONIC_ELEMENTS) are never bonded, so each cation is a molecule of its own; halide anions come out as single atoms;

  • an atom has at most max_bonds bonds by element (H: 1), the shortest kept;

  • the molecules are the connected components, numbered in the order of their first atom, so O H H O H H ... water gives index // 3.

Inside SEAMM, molecules_of_configuration() uses a configuration’s own bonds when it has them. The test suite checks this module against molsystem wherever molsystem is installed, so the two cannot drift apart.

Only ASE and numpy are needed.

active_learning_step.pool module#

Candidate pools: the frames of extxyz and SDF files, as records and as structures.

A pool file is split into records, the exact bytes of each frame, so that a selection can be written as a verbatim subset of the input. Rewriting frames through ASE would drop keys it does not understand (for example the DDEC6 charges and the SEAMM header keys), and the labelling flowcharts read these files directly.

  • extxyz: a record is the atom-count line, the comment line and that many atom lines.

  • SDF: a record runs through its $$$$ line; everything is kept, including the blank line that ends each property.

  • Either may be gzipped (.extxyz.gz, .sdf.gz); the records are the uncompressed bytes.

Each record is also turned into an ase.Atoms with an info dictionary holding the configuration name and the total charge:

  • extxyz frames are parsed by ASE, one record at a time, so the frame numbering always matches the records;

  • SDF records written by SEAMM carry the full-precision coordinates (SEAMM|XYZ|json|), the cell (SEAMM|cell|json|, as a, b, c, alpha, beta, gamma) and the net charge (SEAMM|net charge|int|); other SDF files fall back to the V2000 atom block and the M  CHG lines.

Nothing here needs torch or xnn.

active_learning_step.samplers module#

Batch samplers for training committee members: one kind per batch, a set cell share.

Adapted from code by Sina Mostafanejad, with the kinds named as in scores.csv (cell and cluster).

  • KindBatchSampler never mixes clusters and periodic cells in a batch, and gives each kind its own batch size: a cell holds about 20 times the atoms and neighbours of a cluster, so one size cannot suit both.

  • ShareBatchSampler keeps one pass over the clusters per epoch and draws the cell batches so that they make a fixed share of the epoch’s batches, cycling through the cells in a fresh order. Without it, the share of optimiser steps spent on cells is set by the selection, and a selection of many clusters and few cells hardly trains on cells.

Both are DDP-aware (every rank builds the same global batches from the same seed and takes its own slice of each) and reshuffle every epoch under any caller: the trainer tells them the epoch with set_epoch (xnn 0.12 does in every launch mode), and they also advance their own epoch after each pass, giving the order set_epoch would. So a sampler passed to an xnn 0.11 Trainer, which calls set_epoch only in distributed runs, still reshuffles.

Only torch is needed.

active_learning_step.score module#

seamm-al score: the committee’s spreads for every frame of a candidate pool.

Each frame of the pool files (extxyz, or SDF from SEAMM) becomes one row of scores.csv:

  • where it is: file, index (0-based, in the file), configuration;

  • what it is: kind (cell if periodic, else cluster), n_atoms, formula, total_charge, n_molecules (cells);

  • the spreads: rho_energy (eV), rho_forces and rho_forces_max_atom (eV/A), and for cells rho_pressure and rho_pressure_molecular (atm);

  • the committee average: energy_mean (eV), and for cells pressure_mean and pressure_molecular_mean (atm);

  • any info keys asked for with carry.

Clusters are scored in batches; cells one at a time, with their molecules from the bond graph (molecules()). Frames are read and written in order, so memory stays small however large the pool.

active_learning_step.selection module#

seamm-al select: choose frames from scores.csv within a core-hour budget.

The candidates are put in order and taken until the budget is spent:

  • Ranking (the default). Within each kind, every frame is ranked on each of the kind’s ranking columns, largest spread first, and the ranks are summed. Cells use rho_pressure_molecular and rho_forces, clusters rho_forces; both can be changed (--rank cell=rho_forces_max_atom). Kinds are merged by the rank-sum divided by its largest possible value, so the best cell and the best cluster come first together.

  • Thresholds (--threshold): only frames with a spread at least the threshold are candidates (the rule of Smith et al.; with several thresholds any one suffices, or all with --combine all). The candidates are then ordered by ranking as above.

Frames are taken in that order. --cap COLUMN=N allows at most N frames per value of a column; --exclude FILE drops configurations already labelled. A frame whose cost would take the total past --budget stops the selection, or with --fill is skipped while cheaper frames further down still fit.

Costs are core-hours per frame, by kind or by group: --cost cell=2800 or --cost cell:EC=2800 with --group-column naming the column that holds EC. Groups are shell-style patterns, and the first match wins.

The selected frames are written as a verbatim text subset of the input files: the records are copied byte for byte, in selection order, one output file per format.

active_learning_step.threads module#

How many CPU threads torch uses: no scheduler-specific code.

The thread count is, in order:

  1. the one asked for (--threads);

  2. TORCH_NUM_THREADS, set for torch alone, e.g. by LAMMPS’s mdi_bind.sh for an engine beside a GPU, which keeps OMP_NUM_THREADS at 1 for everything else;

  3. OMP_NUM_THREADS, which SEAMM (seamm_exec) sets for the codes it runs from the job’s allocation, as do PBS and most launchers;

  4. the CPUs this process may run on (its affinity mask), which Slurm and PBS cpusets, taskset and containers restrict; on a plain machine, all of them.

So it works the same under Slurm, PBS, SEAMM’s own JobServer or no scheduler at all, and never uses more cores than the process was given.

active_learning_step.train module#

seamm-al train: the k members of a committee, each on k - 1 folds of the data.

The labelled frames of the data files are cut into k folds (kfold_indices(), seeded by the data seed); member l trains on every fold but l and validates on fold l, from the initialisation seed init_seed + l. The recipe (model, loss weights, optimiser, epochs, the recorded subtracted dispersion) is an ordinary xnn YAML config; only the data, the folds, the seeds, the output directory and the batching come from here.

Batches never mix clusters and cells (samplers): clusters use the config’s data.batch_size, cells cell_batch_size. With cell_share the cell batches make that share of every epoch. With steps every member trains for that many optimiser steps whatever the size of the data (the epochs are the steps divided by the batches per epoch), so committees trained on different selections differ only in their data.

The output directory holds

  • manifest.json: the folds’ sizes, the seeds, the data files with their SHA-256, a hash of the ordered frames, the recipe, the batching and the code versions. Every member checks it before training, so members trained in separate jobs are certain to share one fold plan; a different plan in the same directory is refused;

  • frames.csv: every frame with its file, index, configuration name, kind and fold;

  • m<l>/: each member’s best.pt, last.pt, history.json and member.json (its seeds, sizes, steps, timing and versions).

A member whose last.pt exists is skipped, so a job can be rerun after a failure.

active_learning_step.train.stratified_fold_plan(strata, k, data_seed)[source]#

Folds that split every stratum as evenly as possible.

The strata are taken in sorted order; the frames of each are shuffled (one generator seeded by data_seed) and dealt to the folds in turn, each stratum starting at the fold after the one the previous stratum ended on. So within a stratum the folds’ counts differ by at most one, and so do the folds’ sizes.

Parameters:

  • strata (sequence of str) – The stratum of every frame.

  • k (int) – Number of folds.

  • data_seed (int) – Seed of the shuffles.

Returns:

The fold of every frame.

Return type:

numpy.ndarray of int

Raises:
